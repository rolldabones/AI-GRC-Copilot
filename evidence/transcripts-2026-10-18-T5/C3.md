# SECTION 1: ONE-PAGE EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**What this is:** Harrowgate Freight's first AI Policy. It sets the rules for every AI system we build, buy or use in the UK and Germany.

**Why now:** We already run five AI systems. Three come from vendors, and one (Vigilo) processes in-cab video of drivers. We have no single rulebook, no complete inventory and no consistent approval or evidence trail. The governance program starts next quarter.

**What it requires, in brief:**
1. **Register every AI system** in ServiceNow before production use.
2. **Classify each system** as High, Medium or Low risk using a short checklist.
3. **Approve by tier.** High is approved by the AI Steering Group (COO chair). Medium is approved by the AI Risk Lead. Low is self-registered by the system owner and sample-checked.
4. **Protect personal data.** A DPIA is required before any AI system processes personal data in a way that meets the DPIA triggers. Vigilo meets them.
5. **Keep humans in charge of decisions about people.** No disciplinary or other adverse action against any person based solely on AI output. An appeal route is required.
6. **Hold vendors to minimum terms.** No training on our data without consent, incident notice, model change notice, audit rights, and data return and deletion at exit. Legal continues to review every AI vendor contract.
7. **Monitor, change and respond under control.** Changes go through Jira, monitoring through Datadog, and incidents and exceptions through ServiceNow.
8. **Be open.** Customers are told when they are dealing with AI. Drivers are told how fatigue monitoring works.

**Who owns it:** The COO is accountable. A Policy Owner [TBD, proposed: AI Risk Lead] maintains it. The AI Steering Group oversees it.

**What it costs to run:** Designed for a two-person risk team. The main load falls on system owners, who complete intake and keep evidence current.

**Top risks if we do nothing:** Unlawful employee monitoring, regulator inquiry on driver video, vendor misuse of data, customer harm from chatbot errors, and no evidence trail for auditors.

**Decisions needed from the COO:** Confirm decisions D-01 to D-07, name the Policy Owner and system owners, and resolve the open items, especially OI-01 (employee representative consultation for Vigilo) and OI-02 (applicable AI law).

### 1. Objective
Ensure every AI system at Harrowgate Freight is known, risk-assessed, approved by the right person, used lawfully and safely, monitored, and supported by retrievable evidence.

**Control objective:** No AI system is used in a business process unless it is registered, classified and approved at the correct tier, and its evidence is current in the named system of record.

### 2. Scope
**In scope:**
- All AI systems built in-house (for example, route optimization and demand forecasting)
- All AI systems or AI features supplied by vendors (for example, Quillon AI, Ledgerline and Vigilo)
- Staff use of generative AI tools for work purposes
- All Harrowgate entities, staff, contractors and temporary workers in the United Kingdom and Germany
- Pilots, proofs of concept and production systems

**Out of scope:**
- Non-AI software (rules-based automation with no learned or generated output). If in doubt, register it and let the AI Risk Lead decide.
- Personal, non-work use of AI tools on personal devices with no Harrowgate data

### 6. Requirements
Each requirement has an ID so it can be tested and referenced in audits.

#### 6.1 Inventory and registration
- **R1.** Every in-scope AI system must have an inventory record in ServiceNow [module TBD, see OI-03] before production use or pilot use with real data.
- **R2.** The record must contain at least the fields in Template T2.
- **R3.** System owners must review and confirm their record at least every 6 months and within 10 business days of any material change.

#### 6.2 Risk classification
- **R4.** Every AI system must be classified as High, Medium or Low using Checklist T3. The highest matching criterion sets the tier.
- **R5.** **High tier** applies if any of these is true:
  - It makes or materially influences high-risk decisions about individuals, including employees and drivers.
  - It performs systematic monitoring of individuals, or processes biometric or special category data.
  - Its failure could plausibly cause physical harm or a safety incident.
  - Legal confirms that a sector or AI-specific legal obligation applies to it.
- **R6.** **Medium tier** applies if it is not High and any of these is true:
  - It processes personal data.
  - It is customer-facing.
  - Its output affects financial records or payments.
- **R7.** **Low tier** applies to all other systems: internal use, no personal data, human review of outputs and reversible effects.
- **R8.** The AI Risk Lead may raise any tier. Only the AI Steering Group may lower a High tier.

#### 6.3 Prohibited uses
- **R9.** The following are prohibited unless the AI Steering Group approves them with Legal sign-off:
  - Using AI output as the sole basis for an adverse action against an individual
  - Covert monitoring of staff
  - Emotion inference about staff or customers
  - Entering personal, confidential or customer data into AI tools that are not on the Approved AI Tools list

#### 6.4 Approval gates
| Tier | Before go-live | Approver | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | Inventory record, risk assessment (Artifact #5), DPIA if triggered, adverse impact analysis, human oversight design, test results, monitoring plan, vendor clearance if applicable | AI Steering Group, recorded in minutes | ServiceNow approval record linked to minutes in SharePoint |
| Medium | Inventory record, risk assessment, DPIA screening, test results, monitoring metrics, vendor clearance if applicable | AI Risk Lead | ServiceNow approval record |
| Low | Inventory record, completed tiering checklist | System owner self-registers. Quarterly 25% sample by AI Risk Lead (D-06). | ServiceNow record plus sampling log |

- **R10.** No AI system goes live without the approval evidence for its tier.

#### 6.5 Personal data and privacy
- **R11.** A DPIA screening is required for every AI system that processes personal data.
- **R12.** A full DPIA is required before processing starts if any trigger applies:
  - Systematic monitoring of individuals, including in-cab video
  - Biometric or special category data
  - Automated decisions with significant effect on individuals
  - Large-scale processing
  - New technology applied to personal data
  - Data about employees or other people in a weaker position
  - Data transfers outside the UK or European Economic Area
- **R13.** System owners must make sure data subject rights (access, correction, deletion and objection) can be fulfilled for data the AI system processes, including data held by vendors.
- **R14.** Data minimization rule: collect and retain only the data needed for the stated purpose. Retention follows the records retention schedule [TBD, see OI-08].
- **R15.** Staff whose data is processed must receive a clear notice before processing begins. For Germany and the UK, consultation with employee representatives must be completed where Legal confirms it is required (OI-01).

#### 6.6 Human oversight and high-risk decisions
- **R16.** High-tier systems affecting individuals must name a human reviewer role with authority and training to override AI output.
- **R17.** No adverse action may be taken against an individual based solely on AI output. The human reviewer must record their decision and reasoning in the system of record named in the system's oversight design.
- **R18.** An adverse impact analysis must be completed before go-live and at least annually. It must test outcomes across affected groups, using methods from Artifact #12.
- **R19.** Affected individuals must have an appeal route to a person not involved in the original decision. Appeals are answered within [TBD] business days and logged in ServiceNow.

#### 6.7 Vendors and third parties
- **R20.** Every AI vendor contract must be reviewed by Legal before signature. This continues current practice.
- **R21.** Contracts must include these minimum clauses, or carry a Legal-approved exception:
  1. No use of Harrowgate data, including driver and customer data, to train or improve vendor models without written consent
  2. Data processing terms, data location and transfer safeguards
  3. Subprocessor list, with notice of changes
  4. Security obligations and evidence, such as independent security reports
  5. Incident notification within [TBD] hours
  6. Notice of material model or feature changes before deployment
  7. Audit rights or the right to receive assurance reports
  8. Support for data subject rights requests and regulator inquiries
  9. Performance commitments relevant to the use case
  10. Data return and certified deletion at exit
- **R22.** A vendor without the minimum clauses and required evidence is **Blocked**. Blocked means no new deployment, no scope expansion and no new data categories until the gap is fixed or an exception is approved.
- **R23.** Full vendor procedures sit in Artifact #19.

#### 6.8 Testing, monitoring and change
- **R24.** Pre-deployment testing is proportionate to tier. Methods, metrics and thresholds are set per system in Artifacts #10, #12 and #21.
- **R25.** Every production AI system must have defined performance metrics, thresholds and an alert owner.
  - In-house systems are monitored in Datadog.
  - Vendor systems must have output-level metrics owned by the system owner, because Datadog may not see vendor model internals.
- **R26.** All model, configuration, prompt and data pipeline changes to in-house systems must be raised and approved in Jira under Artifact #20.
- **R27.** Vendor-notified model changes must be logged in Jira and assessed before acceptance.

#### 6.9 Incidents
- **R28.** AI incidents and suspected incidents must be reported in ServiceNow without delay.
  - Examples: harmful output, data leak, discriminatory outcome, safety-relevant failure, vendor breach.
- **R29.** Triage, containment, communications and regulator contact follow Artifact #18.
- **R30.** No one contacts a regulator about an AI matter without a joint decision by Legal and the COO.

#### 6.10 Transparency
- **R31.** Customer-facing AI must tell users clearly that they are interacting with AI, and must offer a route to a human.
- **R32.** Staff affected by AI monitoring or decision support must be told what the system does, what data it uses and how to challenge outcomes.
