## 1. One-page Executive Summary

**What this is:** The charter that gives Harrowgate Freight's AI Governance Committee (AIGC) its authority. It replaces the informal monthly steering group with a formal body that has defined members, quorum, decision rights, escalation and records.

**Why it matters now:**
- Harrowgate uses 5 AI systems. Three are from vendors (Quillon, Ledgerline, Vigilo). One (Vigilo) processes in-cab video of drivers in the UK and possibly Germany.
- Without a charter, no one can show who approved these systems, on what evidence or with what conditions.
- The governance program starts next quarter with a two-person risk team, so the design must be lean.

**What the charter does:**
- Makes the COO accountable chair with final approval on Medium and High-tier AI systems.
- Gives Legal and Privacy a documented sign-off gate and the AI Risk Lead a non-objection right on High-tier items.
- Delegates Low-tier approvals to the AI Risk Lead to protect committee time.
- Allows any system owner, the AI Risk Lead or the COO to suspend an AI system immediately for safety, privacy or legal triggers.
- Records every decision in ServiceNow and every minute in SharePoint.
- Reports to the Board quarterly, with immediate notice of Severity 1 AI incidents.

| Driver-fatigue camera alerts | Vigilo | **High** | Employee video monitoring, safety, possible employment consequences |
| Route optimization | In-house | Medium | Operational and safety impact. Rises to High if it sets driver hours |
| Customer-service chatbot | Quillon AI | Medium | Customer-facing, personal data in chats, transparency |
| Invoice data extraction | Ledgerline | Medium | Financial accuracy, contact personal data |
| Demand forecasting | In-house | Low | Assumed aggregate business data. Unknown whether personal data is used |

### 2.1 Purpose

The AI Governance Committee (AIGC) exists to ensure that every AI system used by Harrowgate Freight:
- has a named, accountable owner;
- is classified by risk tier;
- is approved at the right level, based on evidence, before use and before material change;
- is monitored, and suspended or retired when it no longer meets requirements;
- is traceable through recorded decisions.

### 2.2 Control objective

> Every AI system at Harrowgate Freight has an accountable owner, a risk tier and a recorded approval proportionate to that tier. Every governance decision is traceable to evidence, an approver and a date. Issues escalate to the COO and, where required, the Board, within defined times.

### 2.3 Scope

**In scope:**
- All AI systems used by Harrowgate Freight Ltd and its operations in the United Kingdom and Germany.
- In-house built systems (currently route optimization and demand forecasting).
- Vendor-supplied systems (currently Quillon AI, Ledgerline and Vigilo).
- AI features embedded in other software where they influence decisions about people, safety, money or customers.
- New AI proposals, material changes, renewals and retirements.

**Out of scope:**
- General IT change management, except where an AI system is affected.
- Personal use of public AI tools. This is covered by #1 AI Policy and #3 Code of Conduct.

**Definition of "AI system":** [TBD, to be aligned with #1 AI Policy]. Until #1 is approved, the working definition is: any software that uses machine learning, statistical models or generative models to produce predictions, recommendations, classifications, content or decisions.

### 2.4 Authority

- The AIGC is established by the COO under authority delegated by the Board [TBD: confirm the delegation instrument, Open item OI-02].
- The AIGC may approve, conditionally approve, reject, suspend or retire AI systems within the decision rights in Section 2.8.
- The AIGC may require any business unit to provide information, evidence or attendance needed for its decisions.
- The AIGC does not override legal advice. Where Legal advises that an action is unlawful, the action does not proceed. Disputes escalate per Section 2.10.
- The AIGC does not set enterprise risk appetite. It applies the appetite set by the Board in #5 AI Risk Management Framework [TBD].

### 2.9 Emergency authority

- The COO, the AI Risk Lead or the relevant system owner may suspend an AI system immediately if any of these triggers occur:
  - risk of physical harm (for example, a route or fatigue alert failure affecting driver safety);
  - suspected personal data breach or unlawful processing;
  - Legal advises the use is or may be unlawful;
  - material incorrect outputs affecting customers, drivers or invoices at scale [TBD threshold, set in #21];
  - vendor security incident affecting Harrowgate data.
- The person suspending notifies the COO within 4 business hours and records the action in ServiceNow.
- The Chair may approve urgent decisions between meetings with written input from Legal and the AI Risk Lead. All such decisions are ratified at the next meeting.

6. Expiring exceptions appear on the AIGC agenda 30 days before expiry.

## 7. Working Habits and Review Gates

**Evidence-first habits**
- No evidence link, no decision. Every decision record links to evidence.
- Unknowns are written as "Unknown/Insufficient data," never filled in by assumption.
- Conditions on approvals always have an owner and a due date.

**Stop rules (any one halts a decision)**
- Meeting not quorate.
- High-tier item missing a Legal, Privacy or AI Risk Lead gate.
- Personal data processed and no DPIA status recorded.
- Vendor system marked Blocked.
- System not in the inventory (#9).
- Suspension method not documented for a Medium or High system.
- Legal advises the use may be unlawful.
- Employee-affecting item in Germany with works council consultation status unknown.
